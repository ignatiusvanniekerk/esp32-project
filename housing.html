Housing

Two-part rounded shell, printed flat and screwed together. Everything is generated from one parametric script, so any dimension can be changed and re-exported.

ESP32 orientation

The board stands vertical against the interior back wall, long axis along X, so the board ±28 mm of length lays flat across the back. The USB connector therefore points at the right-hand side wall, which is where the cable slot is cut. The four M3 standoffs hold it at (±21, ±12) — 42 mm apart horizontally, matching the board's mounting hole pattern.

The cable slot is deliberately generous (12 × 14 × 16 mm). It assumes a standard USB-A plug and a micro-USB/USB-C pigtail ~10 mm from the connector body. If your cable is fatter, open the STL in your slicer and drag that one cutout wider — you do not need to re-run the script.

Speaker grille

The rear is perforated with a centre hole plus three concentric rings of 3 mm holes at radii 6.5, 11 and 15 mm — far more open area than the original 3×3 dot grid, so sound passes with noticeably less restriction. A 40 mm ring holds the speaker against the outside.

Re-running the generator

Edit the constants at the top of generate_housing.py and run it to re-export both STLs into housing/. Requires Python with trimesh, manifold3d and shapely.

Two things that will bite you if you edit it. First, trimesh's extrude_polygon always spans from z = 0 to height — a cut meant to sit at z = 15.5 needs an explicit translation, or it lands at the bottom of the model. Second, batch your unions and subtractions into single calls; feeding many at once fails with Not all meshes are volumes! inside manifold.
