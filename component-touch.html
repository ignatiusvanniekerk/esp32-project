TTP223 touch sensor

Capacitive touch pad — 3 wires · tap or stroke the top of the pet to wake it

What it is. A small capacitive touch controller. Your finger is conductive enough to change the capacitance of its pad, and the TTP223 turns that into a clean digital HIGH or LOW. This is how you pet it — stroke the top of the housing and it reacts.

4-pin variant. Some TTP223 boards have a fourth pin (TP or OUT duplicate) or a LED pin. The wiring above assumes the common 3-pin module (VCC / GND / OUT). Check the silkscreen on yours before wiring.

2. Diagram — pin locations

DevKit — GPIO32

GPIO32 sits on the right row. It is a normal bidirectional GPIO — an internal pull-up is available with INPUT_PULLUP.

Mini / C3 — GPIO3

GPIO3 is a normal GPIO on the C3 and is also an ADC1 pin, so it can do more if you need it to.

3. Sketch pins

Normal DevKit

#define TOUCH_PIN 32

ESP32 Mini / C3

pinMode(TOUCH_PIN, INPUT);
digitalRead(TOUCH_PIN);   // HIGH = touched

The solder bridge next to the pad selects this. The default A position is what the table above assumes.

5. Sensitivity

Touching the bare PCB pad is unreliable. Glue the sensor to the inside of the top wall so the pad faces the plastic, then touch the outside of the housing. Keep the sensor 2–3 mm away from any metal or wire and solder blob — those act as antennas and make it fire on its own.

Watch out.
  • If OUT reads HIGH permanently with nothing touching it, the pad is too close to ground, a wire, or the USB cable. Move it.
  • The touch pad and the ToF sensor can both react to the same hand movement. Debounce the touch input so one stroke does not trigger twice.
  • Do not mount the sensor directly against the ESP32 board — the regulator switching noise will trigger it.
Test. Flash a sketch printing digitalRead(TOUCH_PIN) and touch the pad. The value should flip cleanly, and return to rest when you let go.
