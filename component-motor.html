Motor + BC337 driver

Movement / nodding — the one module needing a transistor · 4 wires incl. a 1 kΩ base resistor

What it is. A small motor (for nodding or bobbing) driven through a single BC337 NPN transistor. An ESP32 pin can only source about 12 mA — nowhere near enough to spin a motor. The BC337 acts as a switch: a tiny current into its base lets the full motor current flow, which is why the 1 kΩ resistor is mandatory.

4. Flyback diode

A motor is a coil. When you switch it off, the collapsing magnetic field pushes a voltage spike back up the wire, which can kill the ESP32 or the transistor. A diode solves this:

Diode orientation

      3V3 (+)                motor (+)
         |                       |
         |                       C (BC337 collector)
         |                     motor
         |                       |
        GND ------------------- E  (emitter)

  Diode across the motor, cathode (the line / banded end)
  to +3V3, anode to the collector/motor side.

The banded end of the diode faces 3V3. It only conducts when the spike reverses the polarity, absorbing it harmlessly. A 1N4148 is plenty for a small motor.

5. BC337 pinout

Looking at the flat face of the TO-92 transistor with the leads pointing down, the pins are B · C · E (base, collector, emitter) left to right. Double-check with a multimeter before soldering — getting these wrong means the motor sits on and the transistor gets hot.

Watch out.
  • The 1 kΩ resistor is not optional. Without it the base current is uncontrolled and you can destroy the ESP32 pin or the transistor.
  • Common ground. Motor GND must be the ESP32 GND. A floating motor supply will make the behaviour erratic.
  • Noise. Motors inject noise back into the rail. Keep the motor and its two wires away from the mic wire, and add 100 nF + 100 µF decoupling across the motor if the ESP32 browns out or resets.
  • Never drive a motor directly from a GPIO pin, on either board.
  • Disconnect USB before rewiring the motor.
Test. Wire motor +, motor −, base via 1 kΩ. Then ledcWrite(MOTOR_CH, 255) should spin it, 0 should stop it. Test this before adding PWM ramping.
